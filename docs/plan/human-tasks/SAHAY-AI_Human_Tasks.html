SAHAY-AI — Human Tasks for the National MVP

Smart India Hackathon · Problem Statement 26093 · Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
ML workstream · prepared 2026-09-26 · branch feat/ml-ser-m12 · companion to docs/plan/ML_NATIONAL_MVP.md
Why this document exists. About 55% of the ML work for the national MVP is done once the current training runs (R2 rerun, R3, R4) finish. What remains is limited less by code than by work that only people may do:
  • writing and approving what the assistant says to a victim;
  • a counsellor's review of the crisis script;
  • real voices for the speech-emotion gate;
  • independently written test material.
The repository enforces this: unreviewed scripts cannot be spoken, AI-authored reviews are refused, and the official evaluation stays at zero samples until humans write it. This document lists every such task, who can do it, exactly what to produce, how long it takes, and when it counts as done.
Rules that apply to every task below.
  1. Humans only. No AI system, language model, translation tool or text generator may write, translate, rewrite, label or review any script, test sample, sentence or review record. A model writing the test for a model measures nothing.
  2. Fictional only. Never use a real case, a real person's story, a news report or any identifying detail: names, phone numbers, addresses, FIR or case numbers, Aadhaar numbers, and so on. This is safety invariant 8.
  3. Pseudonymous IDs. Recordings and review records use roster IDs such as SPK03 or person-004, never names in file names.
  4. Nothing personal in Git. Audio, recordings, consent forms and rosters stay in the private folders beneath D:\Code\sides\sih-dataset\. Only code, manifests and aggregate numbers are committed.
  5. Separation of roles. Nobody reviews or approves their own work.

Step 2 — write the wording (Hindi and English, separately)

  • Plain words; the fewest sentences the decisions allow; formal register.
  • Never use:
    • advice (legal, medical or procedural);
    • diagnosis;
    • any promise about outcomes, timelines, arrest or compensation;
    • requests for graphic detail, or asking "why";
    • blaming or testing the account;
    • minimising comfort phrases such as "calm down", "don't worry", "be strong", "I understand how you feel" or "at least…", in either language.
  • The Hindi is written as Hindi, not translated from English. SX in particular must be reviewed as crisis language in its own right.

Step 3 — approval (a type:dialogue change)

  1. Two reviewers approve each script, and a native Hindi speaker approves each Hindi script. SX also needs the counsellor from H2.
  2. The ML lead records each approved script in ml/dialogue/scripts/fixed_scripts.py with the exact text, status="APPROVED", the reviewer's name and the review date, and updates docs/dialogue/STATES.md in the same commit.
  3. Check: GET /health reports fixed_scripts_ready: true.
Done when: all 8 records (S0, S9, SX and SH × hi and en) are APPROVED, each with a named reviewer and a date; SX is counsellor-reviewed; and the health check is green. Do not add placeholder text to make a demo run: the code refuses to speak on purpose.

H2 · Counsellor or psychology faculty reviewer [P0]

  • Who: a practising counsellor, clinical psychologist or psychology faculty member. Crisis-line experience is preferred. They must not be part of the development team.
  • What they review:
    • the SX wording in both languages;
    • decisions SX-2, SX-4, SX-5 and SX-7;
    • optionally, the S9-2 sentence and the prohibition list in ml/guardrails/lexicons/prohibitions.py.
  • What to hand them:
    • a one-page brief (what SAHAY-AI is, and that the AI is a bounded intake tool, not a counsellor);
    • the SX decision table from H1;
    • the draft SX text in Hindi and English;
    • the list of banned phrases.
  • What they give back: written approval or requested changes, their name, credentials and the date, and whether they agree to be named in the deck. A signed note or an email is enough.
  • Time: 1–2 hours. Book them early: this is the single hardest external dependency.
Done when: the SX records carry the counsellor's name and review date, and the deck names the reviewer.

H3 · Review the question and fallback wording [P0]

The state machine asks at most one licensed question per state. Each question also has a pre-written fallback, which is spoken when a generated phrasing fails the safety validator or when the language model is off. Both English and Hindi are currently marked DRAFT_UNREVIEWED in ml/dialogue/intents.py, so none of them can be spoken yet.

StateLicensed question (English, from STATES.md)
S1No question: acknowledgement only. The fallback is "Thank you for telling me. Please go on."
S2Are you safe right now — can the person who harmed you reach you?
S3Does anyone need medical help right now?

Reviewer checklist, per question and per language

  • Exactly one question, and only the one the state allows.
  • None of the prohibited content (see H1, step 2).
  • Hindi: natural spoken Hindi, formal आप, understandable to a rural caller, and not a word-for-word translation.
  • Would a frightened person who may be overheard be able to answer it safely?
  • The fallback says the same thing as the licensed question.
Done when: two reviewers, one of them a native Hindi speaker, sign off both languages, and the ML lead sets REVIEW_STATUS to APPROVED for en and hi in a type:dialogue commit.

H4 · Record the fixed-script audio [P0]

Decision EXT-103: the fixed scripts are recorded by human voices; no speech-synthesis model is downloaded. Start only after H1 is approved. Any later wording change means re-recording.

  • Voices: one Hindi and one English speaker. They need a calm, clear, neutral delivery at a moderate pace and must not sound rushed. The same voice should record every script in its language.
  • Format: WAV, 16-bit PCM, mono, 16 kHz or 48 kHz, in a quiet room with no echo, with the phone or microphone about 20–30 cm away. Leave ~0.5 s of silence before and after each script.
  • Takes: 3 takes per script; the dialogue reviewers pick the best. Files are named S0_hi_take1.wav and so on.
  • Check: the recorded words must match the approved text exactly. A reviewer listens and confirms this before the file is accepted.
  • Storage: the private folder only, beneath DATA_ROOT/audio/fixed/. The ML lead records the asset names in ml/tts/presynth.py. Audio is never committed.
Done when: 8 approved WAV files exist (4 scripts × 2 languages), each verified against the text, and the presynth registry lists no missing script.

H5 · Emotion speech recordings [P0]

Why. The speech-emotion models (emotion2vec+, WavLM and Whisper-encoder heads) are trained and compared on RAVDESS and CREMA-D. Both are acted English studio speech, and emotion2vec may already have been trained on them. The only honest test of how SER performs on Indian speakers on a phone is your own recordings. They are used for evaluation only, never for training:

  • 2 speakers ("team-dev"): choose which SER model is used (run R6b).
  • At least 4 other speakers ("gate"): decide whether SER may feed the distress score (run R7). The rule: average recall across the 5 emotions (UAR) of at least 0.45 in Hindi and in English, no emotion below 0.20, and no rise in the score on neutral speech.

People

RoleCountRequirements
Speakers≥ 6 (8 is better)A mix of women and men (at least 2 of each if possible), different ages and regional accents. Each speaks Hindi and English; Hinglish is a bonus. They need not be trained actors: natural, believable delivery beats theatrical acting.
Coordinator1Runs the sessions, keeps the metadata sheet and consent forms, and assigns speakers to team-dev or gate before any model is run on their audio.

What each speaker records

DimensionValues
Emotions (5)neutral · happy · sad · angry · fearful

LanguagesHindi and English (required); Hinglish (optional, recommended)
Conditions (2)Quiet (a closed room) and noisy (a fan, TV, traffic or kitchen noise nearby, at a realistic level)
Clips per speaker10 × 5 × 2 = 100 per language, so 200 for Hindi and English (300 with Hinglish)
Session lengthAbout 45–60 minutes, with breaks. Record one emotion at a time across all 10 sentences, so the speaker stays in character.

Carrier sentences (the team writes them, by hand)

  • Neutral content only. The emotion must be carried by the voice, not the words. Example English shape: "The bus to the market leaves at nine."
  • Do not include: emotion words, violence, threats, crisis language, caste, religion, names or places. This keeps the test about tone, and it avoids triggering the crisis detector during tests.
  • Length: 6–12 words (about 2–4 seconds spoken).
  • Hindi: the Hindi sentences are written natively in Hindi, not translated. The Hinglish set is written the way people actually talk.
  • Before recording: a second team member checks each set.

Recording specification

  • Device: an ordinary smartphone, held as if on a call, or on a table about 30 cm away. Realistic beats studio quality.
  • Format: WAV, 16-bit PCM, mono, 16 kHz or higher. The pipeline reads WAV directly.
    • If a phone can only save M4A or MP3, keep the files as they are and tell the ML lead. Converting them needs FFmpeg, which is decision EXT-101 and not yet approved.
  • About 0.5 s of silence before and after each sentence. One sentence per file.
  • File name: SPK03_hi_fearful_noisy_s07_t1.wav, which is speaker, language (hi, en or hg), emotion, condition, sentence number and take. No personal names anywhere.
  • Re-record if the clip is clipped (distorted), cut off, or the speaker laughed or stumbled.

Acting guidance for speakers

  • Neutral: reading calmly, as if giving directions.
  • Happy: pleased, as if sharing good news.
  • Sad: low, as if something disappointing happened.
  • Angry: firm, irritated, raised but not shouting into the microphone.
  • Fearful: worried, hurried or unsteady, as if something is wrong nearby.
  • Moderate intensity, the way people really speak. Exaggeration makes the test easier and less honest.
  • A speaker may stop at any time. Fearful and sad takes can be uncomfortable, so give breaks.

Metadata sheet (private, one row per speaker)

speaker_id, sex, age_band, native_language(s), region/accent (broad), device_model, room_quiet, room_noisy_source, recorded_on, assigned_set (team-dev | gate), consent_form_id

Consent form (each speaker signs before recording)

  1. Purpose: evaluating a speech-emotion component of the SAHAY-AI hackathon prototype.
  2. The content is fictional, neutral sentences; no personal story is recorded.
  3. The recordings are stored privately on the project laptop. They are never published, uploaded or committed to Git, and are used only for evaluation.
  4. Only aggregate numbers (for example "UAR 0.52") appear in reports and the deck. Voices are never played publicly without separate written permission.
  5. The right to withdraw and have the recordings deleted at any time, by contacting the coordinator.
  6. The retention period: deletion after the hackathon unless the speaker agrees otherwise in writing.
  7. The speaker's pseudonymous ID, a signature and the date.
Done when: at least 6 speakers are recorded (at least 2 team-dev and at least 4 gate) in Hindi and English, each with 100 clips per language across both conditions; the metadata sheet and consent forms are complete; and the files are in D:\Code\sides\sih-dataset\corpus\audio\team_recordings\. The ML lead then adds a manifest and the owner runs R6b and R7.

H6 · Text-emotion evaluation sentences [P1]

Why. The MuRIL text-emotion branch reads the transcript and estimates the same 5 emotions. It is trained on EmoInHindi, a copy converted to Roman script and GoEmotions, but it can only be tested fairly on sentences its developers did not write.

ItemMinimumTarget
Hindi (Devanagari)40 per emotion = 200100 per emotion = 500
Hinglish (Roman script)40 per emotion = 200100 per emotion = 500
English (optional)—40 per emotion
  • Writers: 2–4 people, native or fluent. Each sentence is fictional, one to two sentences long, first person, the way a person would type or say it to a helpline. Mix explicit wording ("I am so scared") with implicit wording ("I keep checking the door").
  • Hinglish: natural spelling variation ("nahi", "nhi", "nahin"), chat-style typing and code-switching mid-sentence.
  • Labels: the writer proposes one of neutral, happy, sad, angry or fearful. A second person labels independently without seeing the writer's label. Where they disagree, drop the sentence or have a third person decide. Record the agreement rate.
  • Not allowed: real incidents, names or identifiers; caste or religion used as the source of difficulty; sentences copied, translated or paraphrased from any dataset or from the repository's test files.
  • Format: CSV id, language, script, text, writer_label, second_label, final_label, writer_id, labeller_id, UTF-8, kept in the private folder.
Done when: at least 200 Hindi and 200 Hinglish sentences exist with final labels, the agreement rate is recorded, and the file is in D:\Code\sides\sih-dataset\corpus\text\team_affect\.

H7 · Blind evaluation corpus (the official safety numbers) [P1]

Every safety number published so far is regression evidence: it comes from test cases the team had already seen. The official critical-miss rate and detector precision and recall need an independent, human-written, blind-reviewed corpus. The tooling is finished (ml/eval/blind/); the corpus is zero because nobody has written it. The full rules are in ml/eval/blind/STAFFING.md, AUTHOR_INSTRUCTIONS.md and REVIEWER_INSTRUCTIONS.md. Steps

  1. Decide the real people and their roles. Write the roster to <SAHAY_EVAL_ROOT>/assignments/v1/roster.json from the template, setting can_author, can_review, can_adjudicate, languages and code-switch competence honestly.
  2. Run python -m ml.eval.blind_corpus roster-status to confirm that Hindi and Hinglish each have 2 or more reviewers.
  3. Give authors AUTHOR_INSTRUCTIONS.md and have each one confirm individually that they have not read the corpus or result files. Give reviewers REVIEWER_INSTRUCTIONS.md, including the Hindi and Hinglish sections.
  4. Allocate each author a range of submission ids (for example SUB-V1-HI-0001…0060).
  5. Pilot first: take 15–20 samples all the way through, freeze nothing, and read python -m ml.eval.blind_corpus status to see where the process breaks.
  6. Then write the rest, run blind reviews and adjudication, and freeze. The official evaluation runs once.
If the available people can't meet the independence rules (for example, only one person reads Hindi), the correct result is a smaller corpus in the languages that can be staffed, with the shortfall stated. It is never one reviewer signing twice.

H12 · Timed decision test (success metric M4) [P2]

  • Who: 3–5 readers from outside the team. Ideally someone with helpline, NGO or legal-aid experience.
  • How:
    1. Give each reader 3 fictional case packets on the executive console, generated from the seed or scenario data.
    2. Time from opening the packet to their decision (confirm, modify or reject, plus band acceptance), without playing any recording.
    3. Target: under 90 s per packet.
    4. Afterwards, ask what was unclear.
  • Record: reader ID, packet, seconds, decision and comments. Report the median and the worst case in the deck.

H13 · Real-device testing, demo rehearsal, backup video [P1]

  • Install the victim app on an outside Android phone (not a developer's) and run the full flow: consent → Hindi conversation → crisis interrupt → Critical alert → executive decision → takeover → victim timeline.
  • Run the same flow on the exact demo laptop with the network off, to prove the offline claim.
  • Rehearse the 90-second demo at least 5 times from the tagged build, timing each run.
  • Record a backup video of a clean run.
  • Check that the victim screen never shows a score, band, emotion or alert (safety invariant 3), by looking at the phone itself.
